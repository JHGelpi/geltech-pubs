# Style guide: geltech.dev

> **Starter version.** These are sensible defaults, not settled rules. Edit freely: the Editor agent applies whatever this file says and nothing it doesn't. Delete anything you disagree with.

## Who I'm writing for

- **Primary:** hiring managers, peers and leaders in data engineering, data platforms and R&D.
- **What they want:** a clear point of view backed by real experience, readable in one sitting.
- **Assume:** technical literacy. Don't assume they know my company, my team or my acronyms.

## Voice

- First person, conversational but precise. Write like I'd explain it to a smart peer over coffee.
- Have a point of view. State it plainly; show the evidence; acknowledge the strongest counterargument.
- Experience over theory: concrete examples, numbers and lessons learned beat generalities.
- Confident, not hype. No "game-changer", "revolutionary", "10x", "secret sauce".
- Humor is fine in small doses; sarcasm isn't.

## Structure

- **Lead with the point.** The first two paragraphs say what the post is about and why it matters.
- Target length: **800–1,500 words**. Longer only if every section earns it.
- Headings every 200–400 words. Headings say something ("Contracts fail at the boundary"), not labels ("Background").
- Paragraphs: 1–4 sentences. One idea each.
- End with a takeaway or a question, not a summary of what I just said.

## Mechanics

- **American English.** Chicago Manual of Style as the tiebreaker.
- **Serial (Oxford) comma:** yes.
- **Headings and titles:** sentence case ("How we cut pipeline costs", not "How We Cut Pipeline Costs").
- **Numbers:** spell out one through nine; numerals for 10+, all measurements, percentages and money (`5%`, `$2M`, `3 TB`).
- **Dates:** "November 21, 2026" in prose; ISO (`2026-11-21`) in tables and code.
- **Acronyms:** spell out on first use unless universally known (API, SQL, AI are fine): "change data capture (CDC)".
- **Em dashes:** sparingly, at most one or two per post. Prefer commas, colons or parentheses.
- **Lists:** parallel structure; no period on fragments, periods on full sentences.
- **Links:** descriptive link text, never "click here". Link to primary sources.
- **Code and names:** tool names in their official casing (dbt, Snowflake, PostgreSQL, Kafka); code, file names and commands in `backticks`.

## Words and habits to avoid

- Corporate filler: leverage (as a verb), synergy, utilize, robust, seamless, best-in-class, paradigm, "at the end of the day", "move the needle".
- Hedge stacks: "I think it may possibly be the case that…". Pick a level of confidence and commit.
- Throat-clearing openers: "In today's fast-paced world…", "Data is the new oil".
- Unexplained internal jargon or team names.
- Rhetorical-question chains (more than one in a row).

## Claims and sources

- Any number, statistic or claim about other companies or research needs a source, linked inline.
- Direct quotes are verbatim, attributed, and linked.
- Opinions are labeled as opinions ("In my experience…"); they don't need sources.
- Dated facts get a date ("as of 2026").

## Titles and descriptions (share cards)

- **Title:** specific and honest; under ~60 characters so it isn't cut off in search results and cards.
- **Description:** 160 characters or fewer; says what the reader will learn; makes sense with no other context.
- No clickbait. The post must deliver what the title promises.
